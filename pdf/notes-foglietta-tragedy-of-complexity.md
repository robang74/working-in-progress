## Step 5 — Analytical Essay

The article is built as an induction machine. Its premise states the rule of the game: since a general/theoretical approach cannot seize attention, the thesis will be assembled from maximally contentious examples — the euro, green energy, Russian biosecurity, American energy purchases. The move is self-aware: the rhetorical strategy exemplifies its own object. Attention is the scarce currency, examples are the competing products, and the article selects for "attention-catching" ones. In the vocabulary of the economic paper it shares a title with, Foglietta is describing the demand side of the attention market while Oehmke and Zawadowski model the supply side.

The first movement (everyday examples) establishes the formal object: not the choice of A over B, but the systematic suppression of the "versus" itself. Each A/B pair is engineered so that the B-position, once heard, reveals a hidden risk structure — the symmetry between misconduct-risk and mediocrity-risk, the antitrust logic that prevention operates top-down before bottom-up, the control illusion that inverts networking and M&A. The claim is falsifiable in process-terms: a healthy debate would at least *stage* the versus; "stadium fanaticism" instead recruits arguments to a pre-committed conclusion. Retrospectively, the iceberg metaphor announced in the first movement is the master key for the second: the higher-scale examples (batteries, plague, diesel) are all cases where the visible peak (a technology, an incident, a trade deal) conceals a submerged mass — domain, scenario, time.

The middle section performs the article's own medicine on itself. "The underlying idea" and the OK/KO block constitute a self peer-review: the France-as-credit-card metaphor is stripped to its defensible core (systemic risk transmission without formal guarantee, with Article 125 TFEU as the legal boundary) and the overclaim (deliberate liability transfer) is marked as the dismissible residue. This is the article's strongest rhetorical gesture: it demonstrates the hypothesis→thesis→confutation→correction cycle on its own opening example, converting a KO into a more precise OK.

The three higher-scale examples then execute the declared mapping. Australia/Italy varies the **domain**: the same battery-plus-renewables solution is refracted through geography, population density, and the Vajont lesson that risk is a function of location, not technology. Siberia varies the **scenario**: a reported lab death is made to support two incompatible readings (accident vs. managed demonstration), and the uncertainty itself is the message — the question "how many other scenarios did you forget to validate?" outvalues any verdict. The diesel deal varies the **time** horizon: the negligible spot-price arithmetic (16 hours of US consumption) is dissolved by a wartime reframe in which buying undelivered fuel is buying an option on time, at a 3× guarantee markup, against rivals who must pay for deliverability.

The coda compresses the thesis to its epistemic floor: opinions are cheap, understanding is not, and societies that reward allegiance over method lose even the awareness that a method existed. The Marcus Aurelius coda is not decoration — it closes the loop opened in the Premise, where the author conceded that examples are crutches for attention; the crutch is needed because the disease (attention scarcity, shortcut preference) is the very subject of the piece.
